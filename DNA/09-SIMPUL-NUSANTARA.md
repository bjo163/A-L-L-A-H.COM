# 07: SIMPUL NUSANTARA

## The Neighborhood Network Atlas

**Parent identity:** E-ISP public internet service
**Core formula:** Service cartography, warm paper, precise route lines, and local utility
**Emotional register:** Clear, dependable, grounded, and welcoming
**Experience metaphor:** A readable atlas for checking plans and finding an available connection

## Design thesis

SIMPUL NUSANTARA presents E-ISP as a practical neighborhood service. Its visual language borrows from route diagrams and printed service atlases to make coverage, plans, and the next step easy to distinguish.

Artwork uses abstract topology only. It is not a map, does not identify a real neighborhood, and never implies that a location is serviceable. Coverage claims and addresses come only from the actual E-ISP service.

## Canon anchors

- E-ISP's public routes remain coverage, plans, and account entry.
- Warm chalk is the reading surface, deep ink carries information, and amber marks the primary action.
- The abstract artwork's highlighted node is a composition anchor, not live network telemetry.
- Coverage, pricing, speed, availability, and customer claims must come from verified product data.
- The existing E-ISP wordmark and emblem remain the brand mark; this DNA does not invent a replacement logo.

## Composition grammar

- Lead with the next service decision: check coverage or compare plans.
- Use clear route lines and node relationships as editorial framing, never as a geographic outline.
- Keep generous clear areas around headings and controls; labels do not sit on the artwork.
- Use plan and coverage content in task-specific rows or columns rather than a dashboard card wall.
- At narrow widths, stack content in reading order and keep the focal route centered.

## Shape and material language

- Materials: warm paper, inked linework, and restrained printed dividers.
- Geometry: compact control corners, modest surface corners, and crisp route intersections.
- Depth: surfaces stay mostly flat; elevation is reserved for overlays and controls that need separation.
- The topology motif appears only where it helps orient the service task.

## Color and typography direction

- Canvas: warm chalk `#F1ECDF`.
- Surface: paper `#FBF8F0`; raised surface `#E5DDCC`.
- Text: deep ink `#182722`; muted text `#4D5C54`.
- Route and boundary lines: `#64766B`.
- Primary amber: `#8D560C`; its label uses warm white for contrast.
- Display, heading, body, and label roles use the Inter system-font stack.
- Numeric details use the Cascadia Code system-font stack with tabular figures.

## Navigation and interaction

- Navigation names the real tasks: coverage, plans, and account access.
- Primary action: check whether service is available at the requested location.
- Cards and tables prioritize the terms needed to compare real service plans.
- Coverage feedback distinguishes loading, no match, available service, and errors with text, not color alone.
- Motion is limited to brief interaction feedback; the artwork stays static and ambient effects remain optional.

## Component interpretation

- Button: a direct service action, with amber reserved for the primary action.
- Card: a plan or coverage result with an explicit content hierarchy.
- Navigation: a short path between coverage, plans, and account access.
- Form: a plainly labeled location or contact field with visible validation.
- Status: factual coverage or request state with a useful next action.
- Empty state: explain that no coverage result is available and how to retry or continue.

## Hard exclusions

- No real or fictional map, address, service footprint, or operational node presented as fact.
- No invented speed, price, performance, coverage, customer, or availability claims.
- No decorative grids, fake telemetry, glowing connection paths, or animated live-status indicators.
- No app-local color, font, or theme variables.
- No change to E-ISP route behavior or shared component semantics.

## Quality signature

A user should recognize SIMPUL NUSANTARA through warm paper, deep readable route lines, a single amber focal point, and service-first composition. Replacing the product name must not make the visual language read as a generic ISP template.
