# 06 — MYCELIUM

## A Digital World That Is Alive

**Parent identity:** Organic, generative, and network-oriented experiences  
**Core formula:** Biological networks × biomorphic architecture × generative systems  
**Emotional register:** Interconnection, growth, adaptation, discovery  
**Experience metaphor:** Navigating an ecosystem that grows through relationships

## Design thesis

MYCELIUM rejects the assumption that every interface must be made from rectangles arranged in rows and columns. It borrows from branching roots, neural networks, membranes, cellular structures, coral, and topographic forms.

The interface should feel alive through relationships and organic transitions, not through random blobs or decorative animation. Structure remains intentional even when the composition is nonlinear.

## Composition grammar

- Use branching, radial, network, cellular, and topographic layouts when they express real relationships.
- Let connected objects form visible clusters; show hierarchy through scale, density, proximity, and path.
- Use fluid boundaries and organic masks for selected surfaces, not every component.
- Provide a clear linear index or list alternative for users who do not navigate spatial diagrams.
- Keep task flows predictable even when their visual shell is unconventional.
- Avoid false biological metaphors when they do not clarify the content.

## Shape and material language

- Geometry: branching networks, rounded nodes, membranes, cellular clusters, fluid contour lines.
- Surfaces: translucent membrane, mineral texture, soft organic matte, bioluminescent accents.
- Depth: overlapping tissue-like layers, connected planes, subtle depth gradients.
- Data visualization: network maps, growth paths, clusters, topographic flows.

## Color and typography direction

- Base: abyssal green, deep mineral, bone white.
- Accents: mineral mint, coral, muted violet, bioluminescent highlights.
- Display: organic humanist or softly expressive sans-serif.
- Body: highly legible humanist sans-serif.
- Data: clear numeric and label styles; no text embedded only in an organic image.

## Navigation and interaction

- Navigation metaphor: network path, branching route, ecosystem map.
- Primary action: connect, explore, grow, follow a path, or inspect a node.
- Selection: emphasize the selected node and its actual relationships; do not animate unrelated nodes.
- Progress: growth or network expansion only when it maps to a real task state.
- Motion: pulse, unfold, connect, morph, or grow; all ambient motion is subtle and optional.
- Provide a conventional list/tree equivalent and a reduced-motion version.

## Component interpretation

- Button → clear node action with an organic frame.
- Card → membrane / cell / connected cluster surface.
- Navigation → living network with accessible linear alternative.
- Dialog → focused node or membrane chamber.
- Tabs → ecosystem branches or categories.
- Progress → growth path linked to measurable completion.
- Table → stable text-first alternative to network-heavy data.
- Empty state → dormant ecosystem with a clear starting point.

## Hard exclusions

- No random blobs presented as a design system.
- No animated organisms that obscure data or consume attention indefinitely.
- No nonlinear navigation without a clear back path, selected state, and list alternative.
- No color-only or shape-only status.
- No biological imagery used as a substitute for meaningful information architecture.

## Quality signature

A user should recognize MYCELIUM through connected organic structures, branching navigation, membrane-like surfaces, and growth metaphors that correspond to real relationships or progress.
